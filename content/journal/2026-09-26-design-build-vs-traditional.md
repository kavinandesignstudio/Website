There are two common ways to build in India. In the **traditional** route you hire an architect to design and a separate contractor to build. In **design + build**, one firm does both. Each has its place.

## The traditional route

**How it works:** the architect designs and prepares drawings; you tender or appoint a contractor; the architect may supervise.

**Good for:** clients who already have a trusted contractor, or very large projects with formal tendering.

**Watch out for:** gaps between drawings and site, change orders, and the classic "the drawing is wrong / the contractor didn't follow it" blame game — with you in the middle.

## The design + build route

**How it works:** one team takes responsibility for survey, design, approvals, procurement, construction, interiors and handover.

**Good for:** homeowners who want one point of contact, renovations and extensions (where surprises on site are common), and clients living away from the site.

**Benefits:**

- **One accountable team** — no blame-shifting
- **Faster** — design and procurement overlap
- **Budget-led design** — cost is considered from the first sketch
- **Quality** — the people who drew it are the people who build it

## Questions to ask any firm

1. Who will actually be on site, and how often?
2. How are variations priced and approved?
3. What is the stage-wise payment schedule?
4. How will I get progress updates?
5. Can I see completed projects and talk to past clients?
