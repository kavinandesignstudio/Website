It is one of the most common questions we hear: *"Our house is 25–40 years old. Should we renovate or knock it down?"* There is no single answer, but there is a sensible way to decide.

## Check 1 — Is the structure sound?

Look for (and ask an architect or engineer to assess):

- Wide or growing cracks in walls, especially diagonal cracks near doors and windows
- Sagging or leaking roof slabs, exposed or rusted reinforcement
- Dampness rising from the floor, salt deposits at the base of walls
- Settlement — doors that no longer close, sloping floors

Hairline plaster cracks are normal. Structural distress is not. If the structure is fundamentally sound, renovation is usually the smarter route.

## Check 2 — Can it take another floor?

Adding a floor is often the reason families consider rebuilding. A **measured survey** and a check of the foundations and walls will tell you whether a new floor — or a lighter one, such as a timber and clay-tile roof suite like S-Gan Villa's — can be supported.

## Check 3 — How tired are the services?

Old aluminium wiring, undersized cables, galvanised pipes and leaking drains are common. The good news: **complete re-servicing** — new concealed wiring and plumbing — is very achievable within a renovation.

## Check 4 — Does the plan still work?

Renovation can move a kitchen, open up a living room, add a bath or change a staircase. But if the plot has changed (road widening, new setbacks) or you need a completely different layout, rebuilding may make more sense.

## Check 5 — Time and money

| | Renovate / extend | Demolish & rebuild |
|---|---|---|
| Typical time | Months | About a year or more |
| Approvals | Often simpler | New building approval |
| Cost | Lower, keeps structure | Higher |
| Rental during works | Shorter | Longer |
| Embodied carbon | Much lower | Higher |

## Our advice

Get the structure assessed first, then design both options at concept level. A good architect will show you the honest numbers — and sometimes the best answer is a bold renovation that makes the old house feel brand new.
