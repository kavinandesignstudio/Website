Air-conditioning should be the last line of defence, not the first. Traditional Tamil homes — with their thinnai, courtyards and tiled roofs — stayed comfortable for centuries. Here are nine ideas we use to make contemporary homes cooler, naturally.

## 1. Orient the house right

Keep long walls facing north and south; minimise openings on the west, which takes the harshest afternoon sun. Place stairs, toilets and storage on the west as a buffer.

## 2. Shade before you glaze

A window shaded from direct sun lets in light but not heat. Use **deep chajjas, overhangs and verandahs**, sized to the sun angle for each face.

## 3. Use jaali screens

Brick or terracotta **jaali** filters light, keeps privacy and lets breeze pass. It is one of the most beautiful climate devices in Indian architecture.

## 4. Plan for cross-ventilation

Every main room should have openings on two sides — ideally opposite. Air needs an inlet and an outlet to move.

## 5. Bring back the courtyard

Even a small open-to-sky court or skylit double-height space creates a **stack effect**: warm air rises and escapes, pulling cooler air through the house.

## 6. Insulate the roof

The roof takes the most sun. Options include weathering course, reflective/cool roof finishes, terracotta tiles, a ventilated second roof, or a terrace garden.

## 7. Choose materials with care

Thick walls, fly-ash or AAC blocks, rat-trap bond, lime plaster and light exterior colours all reduce heat gain.

## 8. Plant on the west and south

Trees and creepers on the hot faces cool the walls and the air around them. Landscape is part of the climate strategy.

## 9. Let hot air out

High-level ventilators, clerestories and ridge vents release the hot air that collects under ceilings.

> Good climate design costs little at the drawing stage — and saves every month for the life of the building.
