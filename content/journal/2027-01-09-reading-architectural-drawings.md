Drawings are the language of building. You don't need to be an architect to understand them — here's a quick guide.

## Floor plan
A view from above, as if the building were sliced horizontally about a metre above the floor. Shows walls, doors, windows, rooms, furniture and dimensions.

## Elevation
A flat view of one face of the building — front, rear or sides. Shows heights, windows, materials and the overall look.

## Section
A vertical slice through the building. Shows floor-to-ceiling heights, staircases, roofs, slabs and foundations.

## Site plan
Your building on its plot, with setbacks, compound walls, gates, parking, landscape and drainage.

## Schedules
Tables listing doors, windows, finishes and fittings — sizes, materials and where each goes.

## Details
Large-scale drawings of specific parts — a staircase, a kitchen, a joinery unit, a waterproofing junction.

## Tips for reviewing
1. Imagine walking through each room — where's the light switch? where does the door swing?
2. Check furniture fits with space to move around it.
3. Look at window positions from inside and outside.
4. Ask for 3D views of anything you can't picture.

> If you don't understand a drawing, ask. It's your architect's job to explain it.
