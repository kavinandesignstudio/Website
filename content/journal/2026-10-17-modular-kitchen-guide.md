The kitchen is the hardest-working room in an Indian home. Heavy cooking, heat, oil and water demand good planning and the right materials. Here's how we approach it.

## Start with the work triangle

Sink, hob and refrigerator should form a comfortable triangle — close enough to save steps, far enough to give working counter between them. Keep at least 600–900 mm of clear counter beside the hob.

## Choose the right layout

- **L-shape** — efficient for most homes; easy corner storage solutions
- **U-shape** — maximum storage and counter; great for serious cooks (S-Gan Villa uses one)
- **Parallel** — ideal for narrow kitchens, with wet and dry sides
- **Island** — for larger, open kitchens; needs good chimney planning

## Materials that last

| Part | What we usually recommend |
|---|---|
| Carcass | BWP / marine plywood near sink; good-quality ply elsewhere |
| Shutters | Laminate, acrylic, PU or fluted glass in aluminium frames |
| Counter | Granite or quartz — heat- and stain-resistant |
| Backsplash | Ceramic/patterned tiles or quartz upstand |
| Hardware | Soft-close hinges and channels from reputed brands |

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Forgetting a **utility/wash area** for heavy vessels
2. Too few **electrical points** — plan for mixer, oven, chimney, water purifier, dishwasher
3. Poor **task lighting** — add under-cabinet lights
4. Tall units blocking **windows or ventilation**
5. No plan for **gas cylinder** or piped gas access

## Light and air

Cross-ventilation and a good chimney keep the kitchen fresh. A window over the sink is still one of the best design moves you can make.
