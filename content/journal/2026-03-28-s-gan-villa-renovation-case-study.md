In October 2025 we walked into an ageing single-storey house in Madhanandapuram, Chennai. The walls were tired, the wiring was old, the rooms were dark and the family needed more space. In March 2026 we handed back the keys to a home that looks entirely new — while keeping the memory of the old one.

Here is how a single team took S-Gan Villa from measured survey to handover in under six months.

## 1. Start with what you have

Demolition is expensive, slow and wasteful. Before drawing anything, we carried out a **measured survey** of the existing load-bearing structure: wall thicknesses, roof slab, foundations, levels and services. That told us what could be kept, what needed strengthening and where a new floor could safely sit.

> The most sustainable building is often the one that is already standing.

## 2. Design it in 3D first

The family saw the house before a single wall was opened. Our 3D proposal showed the new street elevation, a first-floor bedroom suite and a spa-like bath. Decisions on the gable roof, the timber gate and the kitchen colours were made on screen — where changes cost nothing.

## 3. Strip back, re-service, rebuild

On site, the house was stripped back to its structure. Old wiring and plumbing were removed and **completely re-serviced and concealed**, floors were re-laid and openings re-planned for light and storage.

The signature move is the new **first-floor bedroom suite under a timber-rafter and clay-tile gable roof**. It gives the street front a warm, traditional silhouette and the bedroom a lofty, naturally cool ceiling.

## 4. Interiors as part of the build

Because interiors were designed with the architecture, the joinery fitted perfectly first time:

- A modular kitchen with fluted-glass shutters and a patterned backsplash
- A floating TV wall and crockery unit in the living room
- Full-height timber wardrobes in every bedroom
- A bath with a freestanding tub

## Why design + build worked here

One team owned the drawings, the budget, the procurement and the site. There was no gap between what was drawn and what was built, no blame between architect and contractor, and one point of contact for the family. That is what made an under-six-month programme possible.
